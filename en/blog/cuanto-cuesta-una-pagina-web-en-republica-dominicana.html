How Much Does a Website Cost in the Dominican Republic?

It's the first question almost every client asks me, and rightly so: before deciding to invest in a website, you need to know what price range you're in. The problem is that "a website" can mean very different things — from a single screen with your contact info to a complete online store with a payment gateway. Here are real numbers from the Dominican market, no beating around the bush.

Landing page: from RD$18,000 (~US$300)

This is a single page built for a specific goal: selling a product, capturing leads for a service, or promoting an event. It includes responsive design (looks good on phone and desktop), a contact form that actually works, and basic optimization for fast loading. It's the most common option for businesses just starting out or launching a one-off promotion.

Corporate website: from RD$45,000 (~US$750)

Here we're talking about a multi-page site — Home, About Us, Services, Contact, and whatever pages your business needs. It's the right choice if you want to project seriousness to clients who look you up on Google before deciding whether to trust you. It can include social media integration, WhatsApp Business, and complete on-page SEO so you show up in relevant searches.

E-commerce or a custom platform: from RD$95,000 (~US$1,600)

If you sell products online, you need much more than a pretty page: a product catalog, a shopping cart, a payment gateway (card, bank transfer), and an admin panel so you can manage your inventory yourself without depending on a developer every time you want to change something. Custom projects (like internal systems or apps with their own business logic) also fall in this range, and the final price depends directly on how many features you need.

What makes the price go up or down?

  • Number of pages and sections: more content means more design and layout time.
  • Custom functionality: admin panels, integrations with external APIs, specific business logic.
  • Custom design vs. an adapted template: a 100% original design takes longer than adapting an existing base.
  • Content: if you already have copy and images ready, the project moves faster (and cheaper) than if everything needs to be created from scratch.
  • Urgency: very tight deadlines sometimes mean an extra cost for work outside normal hours.

Watch out for "too good" offers

It's tempting to accept a quote much lower than the rest, but in web development that almost always means one of these: generic templates with no real customization, code you won't be able to maintain or scale later, or a project that ends up half-finished because the price didn't cover the real work. A well-built website is an investment that keeps bringing you clients for years — it's worth treating it as one.
